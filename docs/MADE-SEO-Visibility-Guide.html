How Google Decides Who Shows Up

When someone in McLean types “botox near me” into Google, three things decide whether MADE shows up: relevance, distance, and prominence. You can’t change distance — but the other two are entirely in your hands.

Relevance
Does your site clearly mention the service the person searched for? If your Botox page just says “neurotoxin” in clinical language, Google may not connect it to the word “Botox.” If your site never mentions McLean, Tysons, or Vienna by name, Google won’t know you serve those areas.
Distance
Google measures how far you are from the searcher. McLean (22101 / 22102) is your anchor. You’ll naturally rank best for searches happening close to your address.
Prominence
How well-known and trusted you are online. Reviews, mentions on other websites, social media presence, photos uploaded to Google — all of these tell Google “this is a real, busy, trusted business.”
The headline: as a brand-new business, you start with zero prominence. The next 6 months are about building it. Everything in this doc points toward that.

Google Business Profile Do This First

This is the single most important thing on this list. Your Google Business Profile (GBP) controls whether you appear in the “map pack” — the three results with photos and pins that show up at the top of any local search. For med spas, the map pack drives more bookings than the regular search results below it.

Set it up properly

  1. Go to google.com/business and claim “MADE Med Spa” If a placeholder listing already exists, claim it. Don’t create a duplicate.
  2. Use your exact business name, address, and phone — spelled identically to the website Even “Suite” vs “Ste” matters. Google notices.
  3. Pick the right primary category: Medical Spa Then add secondary categories: Skin Care Clinic, Laser Hair Removal Service, Wellness Center
  4. Add every service you offer, with prices where possible Pull straight from your service menu — Botox, fillers, Aerolase, microneedling, etc.
  5. Write a compelling business description (~750 characters) Mention McLean, the surrounding areas, your nurse credentials, and that you treat all skin types
  6. Set your hours (must match the website)
  7. Upload 15+ photos: storefront, lobby, treatment rooms, your headshot, products Add new photos every 1–2 weeks. Google rewards active profiles.

Then keep it alive

  • Post a Google Business update once a week A new product, a special, an educational tip — anything counts
  • Respond to every review within 48 hours Both 5-star and any negative ones. Google watches response rate.
  • Add new photos regularly Behind-the-scenes, treatment room, before-and-after (with consent)
  • Ask every happy client to leave a Google review Reviews are the #2 ranking factor after the profile itself
Quick win: after each new client visit, text them a direct link to leave a Google review. Your goal for month one is 10+ reviews. Most local searches show businesses with 4.5+ stars and 25+ reviews above businesses with fewer.

Reviews & Listings

After your Google Business Profile, reviews and consistent listings across the web are the next biggest ranking factor for local search. Both compound over time.

Reviews: build a flywheel

  • Text every happy client a direct review link within 24 hours of their visit Strike while the experience is fresh. Same-day works best.
  • Use a short, friendly script — not corporate “Hi Sarah! So glad you came in today. If you have a moment, a quick Google review means the world to a small business like ours: [link]”
  • Respond to every review — positive and negative Thank positives by name. Respond to negatives professionally without defensiveness.
  • Spread reviews across platforms: Google first, then Yelp, then RealSelf Don’t ask 50 people for Yelp reviews in a week — Yelp filters those

Why this helps your SEO (the indirect win)

Instagram links don’t directly pass “link juice” the way a backlink from a news site would. But Instagram still helps in two big ways:

  • It drives branded searches — people Googling “Nurse Karlyne” or “MADE Med Spa McLean” Branded search volume is one of the strongest signals Google uses to evaluate prominence
  • It builds credentialed personal authority — you on camera, explaining treatments, with BSN/RN behind your name For health/medical content, Google heavily favors verifiable practitioner expertise

Content that works double duty

The educational content you’re already making for Instagram is gold for the website too. Consider:

  • Embedding selected reels on relevant service pages Adds depth and dwell time, both ranking signals
  • Repurposing a reel transcript as an FAQ answer on the site Same content, two places, both work for you

Do You Need a Blog?

Short answer: not really, and we’re not building one. Here’s why — and what to do instead.

Why we’re skipping a blog

A blog only works if you publish good content consistently — minimum twice a month, every month, for a year before SEO results kick in. That’s a part-time job. Most med spa blogs end up with three abandoned posts from launch and look worse than no blog at all.

What we’re doing instead

Rich service pages
Each major service page on the site is structured like a mini-article: the treatment, who it’s for, what to expect, common questions. Google rewards depth on service pages even more than on blog posts, because they show clear commercial intent.
A robust FAQ section
Every question a client has ever asked you in person is a question someone is Googling. Add them all to the FAQ section — this is admin-editable, takes 2 minutes per question, and ranks for the “Tier 3” long-tail searches on the keyword page.
Real client photos & testimonials
Original images and authentic testimonials on the site are stronger SEO signals than any blog post. They prove this is an active, real practice. Add new ones as you collect them.
If you ever change your mind on a blog, we can add one. But the bar should be: you’re willing to commit to one post a week for at least 6 months. Anything less and we’re wasting effort.

Earning Backlinks (Without an Agency)

A “backlink” is when another website links to yours. Google treats these like votes of confidence — the more reputable sites that link to MADE, the more Google trusts you. You don’t need 100. You need 10–20 good ones.

Easy first backlinks (do these in month one)

  • McLean Chamber of Commerce membership listing
  • Tysons Regional Chamber listing
  • Aerolase’s “find a provider” directory Email Aerolase — manufacturers usually love listing their providers
  • SkinPen, Galderma, Allergan, Merz provider directories Same as above — most product brands maintain these
  • Yelp, RealSelf, Healthgrades profile links (count as citations and backlinks)
  • Your LinkedIn personal page linking to mademedspa.com
  • Local business directories: Northern Virginia Magazine’s online directory, Washingtonian’s health directory

Higher-effort backlinks (months 2–6)

  • Cross-promote with complementary local businesses Hair salons, dermatologists, plastic surgeons, fitness studios, wedding planners. Mutual referral page = mutual backlinks.
  • Get featured in a local lifestyle blog or podcast McLean Today, Northern Virginia Magazine online, local women-in-business features
  • Speak at a local event or chamber meeting Event recap posts almost always link back to speakers
  • Sponsor or donate to a local charity event Sponsor list pages typically include a backlink
  • Pitch yourself for “black-owned business” or “women-owned business” features Local DEI directories actively seek listings — you fit the profile
What to ignore: any agency emailing you offering “100 backlinks for $200.” They’re scams — spammy backlinks now actively hurt rankings. Quality over quantity, every time.

Your First 90 Days

A realistic, prioritized rollout. Don’t try to do everything in week one.

Days 1–7 — Foundation

  • Claim & fully complete your Google Business Profile
  • Submit Yelp, Apple Maps, Bing Places, Facebook Business listings
  • Add Google Analytics & Google Search Console to the website We’ll set these up together — takes ~15 minutes
  • Update Instagram bio with website link & McLean location
  • Edit footer copy to name your service areas

Days 8–30 — Reviews & service pages

  • Set up an automated text-message review request after every appointment
  • Aim for 10+ Google reviews in the first month
  • Edit the page title & meta description on every page using the formula in this doc
  • Write 150–300 word descriptions for your top 8 services Botox, fillers, microneedling, laser hair removal, hydrafacial, IV therapy, GLP-1, Aerolase
  • Add 10+ FAQs based on real client questions
  • Submit your business to RealSelf, Healthgrades, Nextdoor

Days 31–90 — Build authority

  • Post on Google Business Profile weekly
  • Continue review flywheel — goal: 30+ Google reviews by day 90
  • Email manufacturers (Aerolase, Galderma, Allergan) to be added to provider directories
  • Join McLean & Tysons Chamber of Commerce
  • Pitch one local feature (Northern Virginia Magazine, McLean Today, etc.)
  • Cross-promote with one local complementary business
  • Add 2–3 client testimonials (with permission) to the testimonials section
Reality check on timing: SEO is a 6–12 month game. You won’t rank #1 for “botox mclean” in week three — that spot belongs to a business with five years of reviews. But by month 6, with consistent execution of this plan, you’ll be in the conversation.
